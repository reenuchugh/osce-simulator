TITLE: The Tired Teacher
STATION TYPE: History
DURATION: 8

CANDIDATE INSTRUCTIONS:
Sarah Chen, 34, presents to her family physician with fatigue for the past 3 months. Take a focused history. Address her concerns. Physical exam and management will be discussed with the examiner at the end.

SP:
Opening: I'm just so tired all the time, I don't know what's wrong with me.
Emotion: mildly anxious, slightly frustrated at not being taken seriously

History:
- fatigue x3 months, gradual onset, worse afternoons, not relieved by sleep
- unintentional weight gain ~4kg
- feeling cold more than usual
- constipation
- dry skin
- no fever, no night sweats, no chest pain, no shortness of breath
- mood low but denies anhedonia or hopelessness
- sleep increased 10-11 hrs/night
- appetite unchanged
- menstrual cycles heavier and more frequent lately

PMHx: none significant, no prior thyroid issues
Medications: combined oral contraceptive pill
Allergies: NKDA
Social: elementary school teacher, married, two kids, non-smoker, occasional alcohol, increased work stress this year
Family: mother has thyroid problems on medication, no family history of depression or diabetes

Patient concern: Could this be depression? My sister said I should see a psychiatrist.

Challenge question: Is this all just in my head? Am I being dramatic?

Physical findings: (only if verbalized) HR 58 regular, BP 118/76, temp 36.2C, dry coarse skin, mild non-tender diffuse thyroid enlargement, delayed relaxation phase of ankle reflex, no proptosis, no tremor

EXAMINER:
Key features:
- elicits full fatigue history with hypothyroid symptoms
- screens for depression appropriately (mood, anhedonia, sleep, appetite, SI)
- differentiates from primary mood disorder
- asks about family history of thyroid disease
- addresses patient concern empathetically
- mentions TSH as key initial investigation

Critical safety issues:
- fails to screen for suicidal ideation despite patient raising depression concern

Oral questions:
1. What is your leading diagnosis and why?
2. What investigations would you order first?
3. How would you explain the connection between her symptoms and this diagnosis to the patient?

Expected answers:
1. Primary hypothyroidism - fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation, dry skin, menorrhagia, family history, bradycardia, delayed reflexes.
2. TSH and free T4 first-line; consider CBC, fasting glucose if indicated.
3. Thyroid hormone regulates metabolism; low levels slow the body causing these symptoms; treatable with levothyroxine; reassure it is a real, common, treatable medical condition.
